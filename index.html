40k 11th edition · house reference

You don't need to know all of it yet.

Everything here is written for someone who started this year. No meta talk, no assumed vocabulary. The official rules are free — this site is the plain-language layer on top, plus the hobby side nobody puts in the rulebook.

At the desk Paint on your hands, model in a clamp.
Painting, start to finish What to buy and how to get your first model painted, end to end. Start here if you have never painted a model. Technique reference Thinning ratios, brush load, order of work, and a symptom-first troubleshooting table. Table-ready painting Fast, sourced methods for getting a whole army playable quickly — not a showcase piece.
Basing Texture, rims, static grass. The step that makes a table-quality model look finished.
At the table Mid-game, someone has a question, dice are waiting.
Battle tracker Command points, victory points, battle round, and what is left of each unit. Saves to your phone as you play.
Turn order The five phases, what you may do in each, and the order operations resolve in.
Charts To-hit, strength against toughness, saves, cover. The numbers you look up twenty times a game.
Keywords Every ability that shows up on a datasheet, in one searchable list.
Before a game Kitchen table, army list open, nobody rolling yet.
Factions What each army actually plays like — identity and playstyle notes, not datasheets.
Building a list Detachments, points, what a legal army actually requires.
House rulings Calls we have made, so we make the same one twice.

Where the real rules live

Games Workshop publishes the 11th edition Core Rules as a free PDF and in the Warhammer 40,000 app. Download them. This site summarises and explains; it does not replace them, and when the two disagree, the PDF wins.

Datasheets for every unit are also free in the app. If a page here is out of date after an errata, fix it in the repo rather than arguing about it at the table.
