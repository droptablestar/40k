Technique reference

Thinning is
the whole
game.

Almost every problem on a miniature traces back to paint that was too thick, or a brush carrying too much of it. If you read one thing here, read the ratios.

One pigment, five dilutions Dark ground → light ground
2 : 1  basecoat
1 : 1  layer
1 : 2  thin layer
1 : 6  glaze
1 : 10  tint

Thin paint barely shifts a dark surface but strongly shifts a light one. That asymmetry is the whole trick behind shading.

Dilution by job

Ratios are paint : thinner by volume, straight from the pot. A starting point, not a law — pigment load varies by brand and by colour, so judge what you see on the palette.

Basecoat

2:1Water or medium

Consistency of whole milk. Two thin coats always beat one thick one — the second evens out what the first missed.

Layer / highlight

1:1Water or medium

Consistency of skim milk. Expect 2–4 passes, each smaller than the last and kept off the recesses.

Glaze

1:6Medium, not water

Tinted water. Medium keeps pigment suspended and stops beading on semi-gloss surfaces. Dry fully between passes.

Wash / shade

1:10Water + flow aid

Shop-bought shades come ready to use. Control the load — a targeted recess wash reads far better than a flood.

Drybrush

1:0Undiluted

Straight from the pot, then remove nearly all of it. Thinned paint on a drybrush is how you get chalky streaks.

Brush load

Three states, judged on a paper towel before the brush touches the model.

  • Loaded

    Paint fills about one third of the bristle length. No bulge at the ferrule, tip still holds a point. Paint driven into the ferrule is what kills good brushes.

  • Damp

    Touch the tip once to the towel. If a bead forms where you set the brush down, it is still too wet for glazing or blending.

  • Dry

    Wipe until the towel takes almost no pigment — streaks barely visible. That faint mark is correct, not a sign to keep wiping.

Order of work

This one really is a sequence. Jumping ahead costs more time than doing it in order.

  1. Clean and assembleMould release, seam lines, fit.
  2. PrimeLight passes. Primer colour is a decision about value, not a neutral step.
  3. Set valuesZenithal spray or drybrush. Establish light and shadow before any colour goes down.
  4. ColourThinned, multiple coats. Over a value underpainting this means glazes, not opaque basecoats.
  5. ShadeRecesses unless you want the tint everywhere. Pull excess with a clean damp tip while wet.
  6. HighlightUp the ridges, each pass tighter than the one before.
  7. DetailsEyes, lenses, teeth, metals.
  8. Base and rimTexture, then a solid rim.
  9. SealMatt overall, gloss only where you want shine.

When it goes wrong

Symptom first — that is how you will actually look it up.

SymptomCauseFix
Chalky, streaky drybrush Too much paint left on the brush, or the paint was thinned Wipe further, use undiluted paint, lighter pressure. A larger stiffer brush deposits more evenly than a small soft one.
Hard tide lines after a glaze Glaze too thick, or the edge dried before it was feathered Thin further with medium, work a smaller area, soften the outer edge with a clean damp brush while still wet.
Paint beads up or crawls Surface is semi-gloss — contrast paints, varnish, or skin oils Thin with medium instead of water. Handle the model by its base. A matt varnish coat resets the surface.
Wash pools on flat panels Too much wash on the brush Load less, then wick the excess away with a dry brush tip or a towel corner before it sets.
Detail disappearing Paint too thick — the classic one Thin and build coats. Once filled, the only real fix is stripping the model back down.
Paint drying mid-stroke Dry palette, warm room, too large an area at once Wet palette, smaller sections. Retarder helps but slows everything else too.
Gritty texture Paint separated in the pot, or skinning on the palette Shake properly — a minute or two, with an agitator. Discard palette paint that has started to skin.
Brush tip splits or hooks Paint dried in the ferrule Never load past a third of the bristles, rinse often, reshape the tip after every clean.
Chalky, gritty finish after priming Sprayed in high humidity, cold, or from too far away Prime between roughly 10–27°C on a dry day, about a foot from the model. A heavy coat of paint will not rescue it; strip and redo.

Consistency words

What people mean when they skip the ratio.

Cream
Pot paint, unthinned. Drybrush and texture work only.
Whole milk
Basecoats. Covers in two passes without pooling.
Skim milk
Layers and highlights. Slight translucency on the first pass is correct.
Ink
Glazes and washes. Colour visible in the mix, no body to it at all.

Rules of thumb

The short list worth memorising.

  • Value does the work. Colour gets the credit.
  • Two thin coats. Then a third if needed.
  • Contrast comes from keeping shadows dark, not from adding highlights.
  • Let each pass dry. Most blotching is impatience.
  • Fix mistakes with the surrounding colour, not by scraping.
  • Finish the model in front of you before starting the next one.
